# Variation

What the supervisor knows about going from one mesocycle to the next. The planner never reads it. It gives principles, never a template: what changes, and how much, depends on the person, on how the last mesocycle went and on what they said.

The goal's ranges are in [methodology.md](methodology.md), under *Ranges by goal*, and nowhere here. Every change stays inside the goal's column there; leaving it is a change of goal, not a variation.

## What can change

A direction changes exercises, reps, rest, or more than one of them. Loads and progression are not part of a direction.

| change | what it buys | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **exercises** | growth spread across a muscle's regions, since different exercises grow a muscle in different places [1], [2], [3]; motivation to train, at no cost in muscle or strength [4] | strength is specific to the movement practised [5], so a main lift that is replaced starts again from less practice |
| **reps**, inside the goal's column | for strength, varying reps and load across a program gave more 1RM than keeping them fixed at equal volume [6] | for hypertrophy, nothing found either way: muscle grew alike across periodized and fixed programs [6] and across light and heavy loads taken close to failure [7]; heavier loads still favour strength [7] |
| **rest**, inside the goal's column | shorter rest buys time and density; shortening it week by week gave strength and size alike to constant rest [8] | longer rest has a small edge for hypertrophy, unclear past 90 s [9]; trained people need more than 2 min to get the most strength [10] |

## Principles

**Vary with a plan, not at random.** Some systematic variation of exercises evens out growth and can raise strength; random variation, session to session, may cost gains [1]. A direction is one theme for the whole mesocycle, not a reshuffle.

**A replacement has to add something.** Different exercises for one muscle reach regions the old one did not [2], [3]: another angle, range or tool, not a variant of the same movement.

**Keep what a strength goal is built on.** Strength is specific to the movement practised [5]; the main lifts are the last thing to change.

**No study sets how much to change.** The trials varied exercises on a schedule fixed by the researchers [2], [3] or at random [4], and their review names a direction, not an amount [1]. How many exercises, how far the reps move, how much the rest shrinks: those are judged from the history, never from a rule.

**Variation does not stand in for volume.** Periodization's edge was found with volume held equal [6]; what a muscle gets in the mesocycle is still set by the goal's column.

**The thread runs across directions.** A direction reads the ones before it: a change that worked is not undone without a reason, and the same change is not repeated by habit.

**What the person said outranks variation.** A joint that hurts, an exercise they want to keep, a goal they changed: these come first, and a direction varies around them.
